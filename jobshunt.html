01 — Background

Background Information

Job hunting in Indonesia is fragmented and exhausting. Seekers toggle between LinkedIn, Glints, Jobstreet, and WhatsApp groups — each with different formats, different quality signals, and no unified application tracking. Meanwhile, mobile-first users are forced to wrestle with desktop-optimised flows on small screens.

JobsHunt was designed as a clean, mobile-first alternative: a focused job marketplace where users can discover relevant roles by skill or title, apply in a single tap using a pre-built profile, and track every application status in one place — no spreadsheets, no forgotten follow-ups.

Goal

Create a job discovery app where finding and applying for a relevant role takes under two minutes — with smart filtering, one-tap apply, and a clear application dashboard.

I usually look through LinkedIn and Glints, but the apps are heavy and full of distractions. I need schedules, salary info, company details, and a way to know my application status.

Gain Insight

Gain Insight

Key insights gathered from user research to guide design decisions.

Easy Apply

Users tend to want it easy to apply for jobs because their own positions are still a lot of students and fresh-graduates with minimal experience.

Job Categorization

Users tend to choose the type of work that is in accordance with their field or background.

Money Oriented

When users choose a job, they usually see and choose a job based on the salary provided by the company.

Location & Distance Concerns

Users tend to think about the distance between their place of residence and their place of work when choosing jobs, especially part-time or internships.

Synthesis

Affinity Mapping

I wrote down all the responses and grouped them by topic. From these insights, I was able to determine the core user needs.

01
Discovery & Relevance
Users want to see only relevant roles — not scroll through hundreds of mismatches.
Skill-tag filtering is preferred over category browsing for junior seekers.
Salary range should be shown before opening a listing — not buried inside.
02
Application Friction
Re-entering the same profile data for each application is the top frustration.
A single-tap apply from the listing card — not a multi-step form — is the ideal.
Cover letter fields deter mobile applicants; optional is better than required.
03
Post-Apply Tracking
Users check apps daily to see if anything changed — and find no update.
A simple status column (Sent / Viewed / Interview / Rejected) satisfies this need.
Push notifications for status changes are strongly desired.

08 — Test

Usability Testing

In order to test the overall quality and ease of navigation throughout the whole design, and to observe areas of errors/difficulties, I went out and had 2 participants interact with my prototype.

Participant Criteria 1

  1. Male Female
  2. Lives in Jakarta and Bandung area
  3. Age 21–30 years
  4. Actively looking for a job
  5. Have applied for jobs through a similar application

Participant Criteria 2

  1. Male Female
  2. Lives in Jakarta and Bandung area
  3. Age 21–30 years
  4. Actively looking for a job
  5. Have never applied for a job through a similar application

Scenario

Sign in using email and play the role of a job seeker — find a UI/UX Designer role, apply for it using your saved profile, then check your application status. When you're done, we'll ask you a few questions about how you can use the prototype.

Summary

  • Completion Rate: 100%
  • Average time to apply: 47 seconds
  • Participants completed the one-tap apply flow easily without any guidance.
  • The UI display is very clean, and the job listings are easy to scan.
  • Participants appreciated seeing salary and work model before opening a listing.
  • Some participants wanted clearer verified-company badges to build more trust.
  • Participants found the application tracking dashboard very helpful and reassuring.
  • Most people mentioned the app felt much lighter and more focused than LinkedIn, and didn't struggle to complete the tasks.
