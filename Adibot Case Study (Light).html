01 — Background

What if the first
interview could run itself?

Fast-growing hiring teams hit the same wall: too many applicants, too few hours. First-round screening still has to be scheduled, attended, and scored by a recruiter one candidate at a time — slow, and inconsistent by the end of a long day.

So we asked: what if the first interview could run itself?

AdiBot interviews candidates on their own time through a link that expires after a few days, then grades each one against a benchmark — above or below average. Only the recruiter sees the score, keeping the assessment honest.

The goal

Build a web platform that lets recruiters run consistent, AI-driven first-round interviews — and automatically grade every candidate so the best ones rise to the top.

Synthesis

Affinity Mapping

I clustered every research insight into three themes that shaped the product direction.

THEME 01Time & Volume
Screening eats the largest share of a recruiter's week.
A role can draw 100+ applicants needing a first call.
Async, always-on interviews remove scheduling pain.
THEME 02Consistency
Questions vary candidate to candidate.
Scores drift as the day goes on.
A benchmark makes candidates comparable.
THEME 03Trust & Privacy
Recruiters want a human to confirm the shortlist.
Transcripts must be searchable & saved.
Candidates must never see their own grade.

07 — Test

Usability Testing

To check flow clarity and spot friction, I had recruiters and candidates run through the prototype end to end.

Group 1 Recruiters

  1. HR recruiter, actively screening candidates
  2. Works at a company of 50–500 people
  3. Runs 5+ first-round interviews per week
  4. Has tried an ATS or hiring tool before

Group 2 Candidates

  1. Job candidate, aged 21–35
  2. Has done at least one online interview
  3. Comfortable with camera & mic on web
  4. Never used an AI interviewer before

Scenario

Recruiters set up a role and reviewed two graded interviews. Candidates received an invite, granted permissions, completed the AI interview, and reached the completion screen — then answered a few questions about the experience.
