01 — Background

What if the first
interview could run itself?

Hiring teams at fast-growing companies hit the same wall again and again: too many applicants, too few hours. A single open role can attract hundreds of CVs — yet the first-round screening interview, the step that filters most of them out, still has to be scheduled, attended, and assessed by a human recruiter, one candidate at a time.

This creates slow time-to-hire, inconsistent questioning between candidates, and scoring that drifts with the recruiter's energy across a long day of back-to-back calls.

So we asked: what if the first interview could run itself?

AdiBot surfaces high-potential candidates automatically. After each AI-led interview, it grades the candidate against a benchmark — flagging whether they sit above or below average. Crucially, only the recruiter sees that score; the candidate never views their own result, keeping the assessment honest and bias-free.

The goal

Build a web platform that lets recruiters run consistent, AI-driven first-round interviews — and automatically grade every candidate so the best ones rise to the top.

Scenario

Rizki runs talent acquisition at a 200-person tech company. Each open role brings 80–150 applicants. He spends most of his week on repetitive 20-minute screening calls, then tries to recall who stood out — often from memory and scribbled notes.

Goals

  • Cut first-round screening time without losing signal.
  • Compare candidates on a consistent, fair benchmark.
  • Keep a transcript and score for every interview.

Frustrations

  • No-shows and endless scheduling back-and-forth.
  • Scoring feels subjective by the end of the day.
  • Good candidates slip through from fatigue.

  1. Job candidate, aged 21–35
  2. Has done at least one online interview
  3. Comfortable with camera & mic on web
